# Journal and language guide

Use these language and section priorities when they affect the requested academic edit. Adapt them to the manuscript, discipline, and current target requirements.

## Scope

Use section function, language conventions, and the requested target format to choose relevant edits. In a literature review, compare evidence rather than merely listing authors.

## Section-function checklist

| Section | Required function | Frequent failure |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Identify content and scope | Slogan, method omission, causal overstatement |
| Abstract | Standalone faithful summary | Vague claims, invented detail, result/conclusion mismatch |
| Introduction | Context to gap to objective | Broad background, unsupported novelty, literature dumping |
| Methods | Reproducible account | Missing unit, parameter, source, eligibility, or rationale |
| Results | Ordered factual report | Interpretation replacing results, unreported statistics |
| Discussion | Interpretation with boundaries | Repetition, one-sided explanation, unsupported mechanism |
| Conclusion | Bounded answer and implication | New evidence, exaggerated application, generic future work |
| Review | Evidence synthesis | Sequential author listing without comparison |

## Chinese editing priorities

1. Make the grammatical subject and topic progression recoverable.
2. Replace vague praise with a concrete object, relation, mechanism, or decision.
3. Reduce nested prepositional openings and repeated “通过……从而……”.
4. Use standard terms consistently rather than cycling synonyms.
5. Keep qualification close to the claim it limits.

## English editing priorities

1. Put the informational subject near the finite verb.
2. Resolve long noun stacks and unclear modifier attachment.
3. Choose tense by rhetorical function and journal convention.
4. Use active or passive voice according to focus, not a blanket rule.
5. Prefer exact reporting verbs: `observed`, `estimated`, `was associated with`, `suggests`, `supports`.
6. Remove empty intensifiers and novelty claims that exceed the evidence.

## Source-corpus corrections

- Do not generate “readability scores,” “logic indices,” graphs, or API integrations unless actually computed.
- Do not insert literature, data, definitions, examples, or cases simply to make prose deeper.
- Do not assume more complex sentences are more academic.
- Do not prefer high-impact sources solely by journal prestige; relevance and direct support come first.
- Do not replace uncertainty with confidence for rhetorical force.
