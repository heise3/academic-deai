# Contextual editing patterns for academic prose

Read the relevant pattern when a passage has a concrete readability or repetition problem. These are editing clues, not a detector, banned-word list, or required series of passes. The structural priorities adapt ideas from Humanizer 3.1.0; see [source attribution](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## Structural patterns to inspect first

| Pattern | Editing decision | Information to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Empty importance framing | Start with the specific supported result or unresolved question | Actual contribution, scope, and comparison |
| Invented “not X but Y” contrast, including across sentences | State the positive claim directly when X adds no information | Real alternatives, study contrasts, negative findings, and limitations |
| Repeated section closers | Remove a closer that merely restates the preceding evidence | New consequences, uncertainty, or necessary transitions |
| Uniform paragraph architecture | Organize each paragraph around its own evidence task | Definitions, methods, comparisons, and synthesis |
| Serial literature summaries | Compare studies by question, design, result, or boundary | Attribution and which source supports each claim |
| Decorative labels and chat residue | Use prose or required document structure | Reporting-standard headings, author notes requested by the user |
| Repeated correction or adjudication | Explain what evidence supports directly | Essential causal, temporal, measurement, and scope limits |

A structural pattern still needs an information check. Do not remove a factual clause just because its surrounding rhetoric is inflated. Do not compress a nuanced comparison into a generic conclusion.

## Weak signals need context

An isolated formal word, transition, passive construction, dash, short sentence, or three-item list warrants no automatic change. Inspect repeated use, redundancy, obscured actors, or a mismatch with the author sample and audience. Neither a keyword count nor a combination of signals proves authorship.

- Change passive voice when naming the actor clarifies responsibility or distinguishes study groups. Methods may reasonably foreground the procedure.
- Keep dashes, hyphens, minus signs, ranges, chemical notation, confidence intervals, and punctuation required by the target format. Revise repetitive rhetorical interruption, not punctuation as a category.
- Keep three-item lists when the data or classification has three items. Do not drop one item to evade a pattern.
- Keep terminology stable. Vary ordinary wording only where it improves comprehension.
- Keep useful uncertainty and statistical reporting verbs. Distinguish empty hedging from a limit supported by the study design.

## Chinese patterns

| Passage problem | Useful revision |
|---|---|
| Generic “近年来，随着……” opening | Begin with the phenomenon, evidence, or concrete unresolved question already supplied |
| Mechanical “此外／同时／因此／综上” chain | Express the actual comparison, condition, mechanism, or implication; retain a transition when it helps |
| Nested “通过……从而……” | Recover the actor, operation, and claimed relation without strengthening causality |
| Vague “具有重要意义” ending | Specify the supported consequence, or remove the empty evaluation |
| Every section ends “仍需进一步研究” | Keep a concrete unresolved question where relevant; let other sections end according to their content |

## Final review

After revising the affected claims, look again for empty contrasts, duplicated closers, forced symmetry, and a newly uniform skeptical template. Review the reader function of retained sentences, rather than requiring novelty in each one. For author samples and conversation-dependent background, read [voice and context](voice-and-context.md).

The optional scripts report observable patterns and content differences. A warning supports review; it does not authorize deleting evidence or forcing a score downward.
