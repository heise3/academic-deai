## Abstract

This study examined the association between carbon dioxide sensor readings and task performance in 72 classrooms during a single teaching session. At the end of the session, a sensor reading and the classroom mean score on a 10-item task were collected in each classroom. Higher sensor readings were associated with lower mean task scores (r=-0.28, P=0.02)[1]. The analysis did not adjust for class size; ventilation rate was not measured, and repeated measurements were not collected. The cross-sectional design does not establish that carbon dioxide caused the score differences.

## Discussion

Higher carbon dioxide sensor readings were associated with lower classroom mean task scores. The classroom, rather than the individual student, was the unit of analysis. The coefficient therefore describes a relation between classroom-level measurements and classroom mean scores; it does not describe a relation among individual students[1].

Class size could be related to both the sensor reading and the mean task score. Without adjustment for class size or measurements of ventilation rate, alternative explanations remain unresolved. Each classroom was measured only once, so the data do not show whether changes in the reading preceded changes in performance. Future work could collect repeated measurements, record class size and ventilation rate, and test whether within-classroom changes show the same association.
